# Exclusivity and scope terms rules

Practitioner + standard-term-sheet-convention rules for when to grant
exclusivity, how to bound its scope, and when to let it lapse rather
than renew it by default.

## Decision rules

### 1. Grant exclusivity only for a scoped, time-boxed diligence/negotiation window, never as a standing default
- **Condition**: when a counterpart requests exclusivity (no-shop / no-solicitation) to protect dedicated negotiation or diligence effort before a deal is signed **Choice**: bound the exclusivity grant to the time needed to complete diligence and produce a firm offer (practitioner convention: on the order of 30-60 days for comparable diligence-stage negotiations), state an explicit expiration date rather than an open-ended term, and require the grant to be renewed by affirmative action, not silence, if diligence runs long **Why**: standard market-convention documents (NVCA, BVCA, VIMA term sheets) treat exclusivity/no-shop as one of the few sections that binds even though the rest of the term sheet is non-binding, precisely because it is a scarce, costly commitment that should map to a specific diligence purpose, not a general relationship-protection clause **Source**: VC Beast, "NVCA Forms: 6 Free Model Documents Explained", https://vcbeast.com/nvca-forms-model-documents-guide ; Duane Morris LLP, "Singapore's VIMA vs. the World – A Multijurisdictional Comparative Review of the VIMA Documents", https://www.duanemorris.com/alerts/singapores_vima_vs_world_multijurisdictional_comparative_review_vima_documents_0920.html **Counter-example test**: an exclusivity clause with no end date ("exclusivity continues until either party terminates this agreement") fails this rule even if the deal eventually closes — the absence of a bounded window, not the outcome, is the defect.

### 2. Scope exclusivity to the narrowest dimension (product line, geography, or vertical) that actually protects the deal's rationale
- **Condition**: when drafting the scope of an exclusivity grant (what the counterpart is excluded from doing with competitors) **Choice**: state exclusivity as the narrowest combination of product line + geography + vertical/customer-segment that protects the specific business rationale for the deal; do not default to "exclusive for all products, all territories" when the deal's own rationale only requires exclusivity in one dimension **Why**: exclusivity-clause practitioner guidance treats scope definition (alongside duration and exceptions) as the primary lever that determines whether the clause protects the intended interest without over-constraining the counterpart; an over-broad scope creates unnecessary lock-in that has to be renegotiated later, which is more costly than scoping narrowly up front **Source**: Sirion, "The Exclusivity Clause Explained: A Guide for Legal & Business Professionals", https://www.sirion.ai/library/contract-clauses/exclusivity-clause/ ; Hyperstart, "Exclusivity Agreement: What it is, Types, and How it Works", https://www.hyperstart.com/blog/exclusivity-agreement/ **Counter-example test**: a regional distribution partnership (rationale: exclusive access to one country's retail channel) that grants worldwide, all-product exclusivity fails this rule — the granted scope exceeds the stated rationale on two dimensions the deal never needed.

### 3. Let an exclusivity clause lapse rather than auto-renewing it once its triggering condition (active diligence/negotiation) has ended
- **Condition**: when the diligence or negotiation period that justified an exclusivity grant has concluded (deal signed, walked away, or gone stale with no active negotiation) **Choice**: do not carry the exclusivity clause forward into the definitive agreement or a "just in case" renewal by default; explicitly re-justify and re-scope it if a new triggering condition (e.g., a renegotiation window) arises, rather than treating "we already have an exclusivity clause" as a reason to keep it **Why**: exclusivity clauses are scoped to protect a specific negotiation window; once that window's purpose is served, retaining the restriction costs the granting party optionality with no remaining protective function — and because removing an existing clause is a subtractive action, negotiators default to leaving it in rather than actively pruning it, the same additive bias documented in subtraction-neglect research **Source**: exclusivity-scope practitioner guidance (Sirion, Hyperstart, above) on duration/exception design; subtraction-neglect mechanism: Adams, G.S., Converse, B.A., Hales, A.H., Klotz, L.E., "People systematically overlook subtractive changes", Nature 592, 258-261 (2021), https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-021-03380-y **Counter-example test**: a definitive agreement that copies the original 45-day diligence-period exclusivity clause verbatim into a multi-year ongoing contract, with no re-justification for why exclusivity should persist past deal signing, fails this rule.
