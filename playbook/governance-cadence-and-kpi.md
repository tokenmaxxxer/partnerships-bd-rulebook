# Governance cadence and KPI rules

Practitioner rules for setting a partnership's ongoing governance
rhythm (steering committee, working group) and choosing which KPIs to
track at which tier, plus when to cut a governance layer instead of
adding one.

## Decision rules

### 1. Tier governance meeting cadence to decision type, not to relationship importance alone
- **Condition**: when designing the recurring governance rhythm for a live partnership (joint steering committee, operating committee, working group) **Choice**: assign quarterly cadence to strategic-direction and top-line KPI review, monthly cadence to policy approvals and cross-functional coordination, and biweekly (or tighter) cadence to tactical execution tracking; do not collapse all of these into a single "monthly partnership sync" regardless of the deal's importance **Why**: a tiered governance structure matches meeting frequency to meeting purpose — strategic decisions need less frequent but higher-context sessions, while tactical execution issues need short-cycle, low-latency check-ins; a single flat cadence either starves execution of timely course-correction or wastes senior time on tactical detail **Source**: Atlan, "Governance Council Meeting Cadence: Tiers, Templates & Tips", https://atlan.com/know/governance-council-meeting-cadence-best-practices/ ; Umbrex, "Partnership Governance and Operating Model", https://umbrex.com/resources/strategic-partnerships-playbook/partnership-governance-and-operating-model/ **Counter-example test**: a $2M/year strategic alliance whose only governance ritual is one ad hoc call "whenever something comes up" fails this rule — no tiered cadence exists for either strategic review or tactical tracking.

### 2. Assign each KPI to the governance tier that actually owns the decision it informs
- **Condition**: when selecting which metrics to report to which governance layer (joint steering committee vs. operating committee vs. working group) **Choice**: report enterprise-level KPIs (e.g., quarterly net revenue uplift, cumulative cash flow) to the joint steering committee; report functional KPIs (e.g., cost-per-lead, integration uptime) to the operating committee that owns that function; report team/sprint-level metrics only within the working group that produces them — do not surface working-group-level metrics at the steering-committee tier or vice versa **Why**: alliance-KPI design guidance separates metrics by which committee can actually act on them; a steering committee shown sprint-level detail either rubber-stamps without real review or burns senior time on decisions delegated elsewhere, and a working group with no visibility into enterprise KPIs can't tell whether its tactical work is moving the metric that matters **Source**: AllianceBoard, "Designing alliance KPIs for impact", https://www.allianceboard.com/resources/performance-metrics-and-evaluation-designing-alliance-kpis-for-impact ; Fiveable, "Key performance indicators for alliances", https://fiveable.me/strategic-alliances-and-partnerships/unit-10/key-performance-indicators-alliances/study-guide/mel2Oov3jfziqU7i **Counter-example test**: a quarterly steering-committee deck that opens with individual working-group story-point burndown before any enterprise revenue/cash-flow figure fails this rule — the wrong-tier metric crowded out the metric that tier exists to review.

### 3. Cut a governance layer once its scheduled sessions stop producing decisions, rather than keeping it on the calendar "for visibility"
- **Condition**: when a review of an existing governance tier's last several sessions shows the same layer repeatedly produces status updates with no decision, escalation, or course-correction made **Choice**: do not preserve that governance layer by default because removing a standing meeting feels riskier than keeping it; fold its remaining useful reporting into an adjacent tier's agenda item and formally retire the layer, stating the retirement explicitly rather than letting the meeting quietly go unattended **Why**: governance structures accumulate additive layers over an alliance's life (a new working group here, a new sync there) far more readily than they get pruned, because removing a standing structure is a subtractive decision people systematically fail to consider as an option even when it is the better fix for meeting fatigue and governance drift **Source**: alliance governance-drift practitioner guidance, The Rhythm of Business, "The Joint Steering Committee as a Beacon of Collaborative Leadership", https://rhythmofbusiness.com/as-seen-in-strategic-alliance-quarterly-the-joint-steering-committee-as-a-beacon-of-collaborative-leadership/ ; subtraction-neglect mechanism: Adams, G.S., Converse, B.A., Hales, A.H., Klotz, L.E., "People systematically overlook subtractive changes", Nature 592, 258-261 (2021), https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-021-03380-y **Counter-example test**: a monthly "alliance sync" whose last six meeting minutes contain only status restatements and zero decisions, still scheduled indefinitely with no retirement discussion, fails this rule.
